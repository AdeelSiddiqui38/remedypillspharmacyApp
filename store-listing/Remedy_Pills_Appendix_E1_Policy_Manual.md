## 1. Purpose and Scope

This Privacy & Information Security Policy Manual establishes the policies and procedures Remedy Pills Pharmacy uses to protect personal information and health information in its custody or control.

The policies apply to all pharmacy employees, managers, administrators, contractors, temporary personnel, and other individuals who are authorized to access Remedy Pills Pharmacy information or systems.

The policies cover information handled through:

- Pharmacy operations and patient records;
- Electronic pharmacy and clinical systems;
- The Remedy Pills application and associated databases;
- Patient communications;
- Electronic files and data imports;
- Paper records;
- Staff computers and other devices;
- Third-party service providers and technology vendors; and
- Other systems or processes used to collect, use, disclose, store, or retain health information.

The policies are intended to support compliance with Alberta's Health Information Act (HIA), applicable regulations, professional obligations, and other applicable privacy and security requirements.

## 2. Privacy Accountability

### 2.1 Privacy Officer

Nayel Anwer, Pharmacy Manager, is designated as the **Privacy Officer** for Remedy Pills Pharmacy.

The Privacy Officer is responsible for:

- Overseeing the pharmacy's privacy program;
- Maintaining privacy policies and procedures;
- Responding to privacy inquiries and complaints;
- Coordinating patient access and correction requests;
- Overseeing privacy training and awareness;
- Monitoring privacy incidents and breaches;
- Reviewing application audit logs;
- Coordinating privacy risk assessments;
- Maintaining and reviewing the Privacy Impact Assessment (PIA);
- Reviewing new vendors and information-management arrangements;
- Coordinating required privacy incident notifications;
- Ensuring privacy policies are reviewed at least annually; and
- Escalating material privacy and security issues to the pharmacy owner/authorized management.

### 2.2 Privacy Governance

Remedy Pills Pharmacy is a locally owned and operated community pharmacy and does not maintain a separate privacy committee.

Privacy matters are managed through the Pharmacy Manager/Privacy Officer, with significant issues escalated to the pharmacy owner or authorized management.

All personnel are responsible for protecting patient information and reporting suspected privacy or security incidents.

---

## 3. Access to Health Information — Patient Requests

Patients may request access to health information held by Remedy Pills Pharmacy, subject to applicable legal requirements and exceptions.

Requests should be directed to the Pharmacy Manager/Privacy Officer.

**Procedure.** Upon receiving an access request, the Privacy Officer will:

1. Record the request.
2. Confirm the identity of the requester using reasonable verification procedures.
3. Clarify the scope of the requested information where necessary.
4. Identify relevant records held by the pharmacy.
5. Determine whether any legal restrictions or exceptions apply.
6. Prepare the appropriate response.
7. Provide access in an appropriate format where possible.
8. Document the response and any applicable reasons for withholding information.

The pharmacy will respond within the applicable statutory timeframe.

Electronic access functionality within the application may support retrieval of patient information; however, technical functionality does not replace the Privacy Officer's responsibility for reviewing and authorizing the response.

Where a request is refused in whole or in part, the pharmacy will document the reason and advise the requester of applicable review or complaint rights where required.

---

## 4. Correction Requests

An individual may request correction of health information held by Remedy Pills Pharmacy.

Correction requests must be directed to the Pharmacy Manager/Privacy Officer.

**Procedure.** The Privacy Officer will:

1. Verify the identity of the requester.
2. Identify the record and information being disputed.
3. Review the original information and available supporting documentation.
4. Consult the appropriate pharmacy professional where necessary.
5. Determine whether the information should be corrected.
6. Document the correction appropriately.
7. Maintain an appropriate audit trail.

Corrections must not improperly delete or alter historical information where maintaining the original record is necessary for legal, clinical, regulatory, or professional purposes.

If the pharmacy does not agree with the requested correction, the decision and reason will be documented and the requester will be advised of applicable rights where required.

## 5. Training, Awareness & Sanctions

### 5.1 Training

All personnel who have access to patient or confidential information must receive privacy and confidentiality training.

Training should address:

- Privacy obligations;
- Confidentiality;
- Collection, use, and disclosure of health information;
- Appropriate access to patient records;
- Password and account security;
- Secure electronic communications;
- Phishing and social engineering;
- Patient requests;
- Privacy incident reporting;
- Physical security;
- Appropriate handling of exported files;
- Third-party information handling; and
- Consequences of inappropriate access or disclosure.

Training will be provided during onboarding and refreshed at least annually, or sooner when significant changes occur.

Training and significant privacy communications should be documented.

### 5.2 Sanctions

Personnel who fail to comply with privacy or security requirements may be subject to:

- Additional training;
- Counseling;
- Restriction or removal of system access;
- Corrective action;
- Disciplinary action; or
- Termination of employment or contractual access, where warranted.

Serious or deliberate privacy violations may also be escalated to the pharmacy owner and/or applicable regulatory authorities.

## 6. Collection of Health Information & Notice

Remedy Pills Pharmacy collects only personal and health information reasonably required for legitimate pharmacy, clinical, operational, legal, regulatory, and professional purposes.

Examples include information necessary to:

- Provide pharmacy services;
- Dispense and manage prescriptions;
- Communicate with patients;
- Coordinate care;
- Process billing and administration;
- Meet legal and professional obligations;
- Maintain appropriate patient records; and
- Manage pharmacy operations.

Patients are informed through the pharmacy's privacy notice and applicable service disclosures about information collection and handling practices.

**Data Minimization.** The pharmacy will seek to minimize the amount of information collected, transmitted, displayed, or retained where the information is not reasonably required. Patient communications, including SMS messages, should contain only the minimum information reasonably necessary. Sensitive clinical information should not be unnecessarily included in SMS or other communications.

**External Data Import.** Where patient information is imported from an external pharmacy system, such as a Kroll CSV export, the pharmacy will require appropriate staff authorization and patient/data confirmation procedures before information is imported.

Any new collection practice or feature that materially changes the type or amount of health information collected must be reviewed by the Privacy Officer.

---

## 7. Use of Health Information

Health information may only be used for purposes authorized by applicable law and consistent with the pharmacy's role and responsibilities.

Authorized uses may include:

- Providing pharmacy services;
- Prescription dispensing and management;
- Patient communication;
- Care coordination;
- Billing and administration;
- Quality and patient-safety activities;
- Regulatory compliance;
- Professional obligations;
- System administration and security; and
- Other legally authorized purposes.

Access to health information must be limited to what is reasonably required for the individual's assigned duties.

Personnel must not access patient information:

- Out of curiosity;
- For personal reasons;
- For friends or family without appropriate authorization;
- For unrelated personal benefit; or
- For any purpose unrelated to authorized pharmacy activities.

The pharmacy will not use patient information for unrelated marketing, analytics, research, or other secondary purposes unless the proposed activity has been reviewed and is legally authorized.

---

## 8. Disclosure of Health Information

Remedy Pills Pharmacy will disclose health information only where the disclosure is authorized under applicable law, valid consent where required, or another lawful authority.

Before making a non-routine disclosure, personnel must confirm:

- The identity of the recipient;
- The purpose of the disclosure;
- The authority for the disclosure; and
- That only the minimum reasonably necessary information is disclosed.

Examples of legitimate disclosures may include disclosures necessary to provide or coordinate patient care or disclosures required or authorized by law.

Staff must not disclose patient information simply because another person requests it. This includes requests from:

- Family members;
- Friends;
- Employers;
- Insurers;
- Vendors;
- Other organizations; or
- Other third parties.

Where consent is required, appropriate consent must be obtained and documented.

Electronic disclosures must be directed to the intended recipient using appropriate safeguards.

---

## 9. Research

**Not applicable** — Remedy Pills Pharmacy does not currently use patient health information for research.

If the pharmacy proposes to use patient information for research in the future, the proposal must first be referred to the Pharmacy Manager/Privacy Officer, who will determine whether additional requirements apply, including:

- HIA requirements;
- Consent requirements;
- Research ethics requirements;
- Data minimization;
- De-identification;
- Third-party arrangements; and
- PIA requirements.

No patient information will be used for research until the applicable requirements have been reviewed and satisfied.

## 10. Third Parties & Information Managers

Third-party service providers that collect, store, process, transmit, or otherwise handle health information on behalf of Remedy Pills Pharmacy must be reviewed before implementation.

The pharmacy will consider:

- The type of information involved;
- The purpose of the service;
- The vendor's security practices;
- Data storage and processing locations;
- Access controls;
- Retention and deletion;
- Incident notification;
- Subcontractors;
- Business continuity; and
- Applicable contractual requirements.

Where an Information Manager Agreement (IMA) or other written agreement is required, the pharmacy will ensure that an appropriate agreement is in place before providing health information to the service provider.

Agreements should address, as applicable:

- Permitted collection;
- Permitted use;
- Permitted disclosure;
- Security safeguards;
- Confidentiality;
- Access and correction support;
- Incident notification;
- Retention;
- Return or destruction of information;
- Subcontracting; and
- Termination of services.

New vendors or material vendor changes must be reviewed by the Privacy Officer.

**Current status:** written Information Manager Agreements are not yet confirmed in place with Railway (application hosting/database) or DigitalOcean (attachment storage) — the two vendors holding patient data at rest. Rather than assuming one is required, the pharmacy will initiate an IMA with the relevant vendor(s) if the OIPC's review of the PIA determines it is needed, and will do so before any real patient data is connected. Google (OAuth), Twilio (SMS), and Gmail (transactional email) carry no health information in their respective flows and are lower risk.

## 11. Privacy Impact Assessment Policy

The Pharmacy Manager/Privacy Officer owns the pharmacy's Privacy Impact Assessment process.

The PIA will be reviewed at least annually and whenever a material change occurs.

**PIA Triggers.** A PIA review is required when there is a material change involving:

- New categories of health information;
- New data elements;
- New collection methods;
- New uses of health information;
- New disclosures;
- New application features;
- New integrations;
- New vendors;
- New Information Managers;
- Changes in hosting or storage locations;
- Significant changes to electronic communications;
- New automated processing involving health information;
- Significant changes to access controls; or
- Other changes that could materially affect privacy.

The Privacy Officer will assess the change and determine whether the existing PIA must be amended or replaced with a revised PIA. Where required, the pharmacy will coordinate submission of the PIA amendment or revised PIA to the OIPC.

## 12. Records Retention & Disposition

Remedy Pills Pharmacy will retain records in accordance with applicable:

- Alberta legislation;
- Health Information Act requirements;
- Alberta College of Pharmacy requirements;
- Professional obligations; and
- Other applicable legal requirements.

The pharmacy's current retention framework incorporates the applicable 10-year ACP record-retention requirement identified for the pharmacy. Where applicable, automated application controls, including the retention functionality implemented through `server/retention.ts`, support enforcement of the configured retention period. Automated retention controls do not replace management responsibility.

Before destruction, the pharmacy will consider whether information is subject to:

- A legal hold;
- An investigation;
- A privacy request;
- A regulatory requirement;
- An unresolved incident; or
- Another requirement requiring continued retention.

Records reaching the end of their required retention period will be securely destroyed or permanently deleted in a manner appropriate to the medium.

## 13. Information Classification

Remedy Pills Pharmacy classifies information according to sensitivity and the potential consequences of unauthorized access, use, disclosure, alteration, loss, or destruction.

| Level | Description | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Level 1 — Public** | Information intentionally made available to the public. | Pharmacy address; general services; public website information; general business contact information. |
| **Level 2 — Internal** | Routine business information that is not intended for public distribution. | — |
| **Level 3 — Confidential** | Business, operational, employee, vendor, financial, or other information requiring restricted access. | — |
| **Level 4 — Restricted Health Information** | Individually identifiable patient or health information. | Patient names; contact information; prescription information; medication information; patient records; health information; patient communications; imported pharmacy records. |

Restricted Health Information requires the highest level of administrative, technical, and physical protection.

## 14. Risk Assessment

The Pharmacy Manager/Privacy Officer will periodically assess privacy and information-security risks associated with pharmacy operations.

Risk assessments will consider:

- Sensitivity of information;
- Volume of information;
- User access;
- System architecture;
- Vendors;
- Data flows;
- Storage locations;
- Electronic communications;
- Physical security;
- Cybersecurity;
- Business continuity;
- Previous incidents; and
- Changes in technology or operations.

A formal risk review will occur at least annually and whenever a significant new system, vendor, data flow, or security concern is introduced.

Identified risks should be documented with:

- Risk description;
- Likelihood;
- Potential impact;
- Mitigating controls;
- Responsible person; and
- Target completion date.

Significant unresolved risks will be escalated to the pharmacy owner/authorized management.

## 15. Physical Security of Data & Equipment

Patient records and equipment containing health information must be protected from unauthorized physical access.

**Staff Devices.** Staff must:

- Lock computers when unattended;
- Use appropriate authentication;
- Prevent unauthorized persons from viewing screens;
- Avoid leaving patient records exposed;
- Protect laptops and removable media; and
- Report lost or stolen devices immediately.

**Paper Records.** Paper patient records must be secured when not actively being used. Paper records reaching the end of their retention period must be securely destroyed.

**Kroll CSV / Data Exports.** Kroll CSV exports containing patient information are considered Restricted Health Information. Before uploading such files:

- Only authorized staff may create or handle the export;
- The file must be stored in an appropriately secured location;
- The file must not be shared through personal email or unauthorized cloud storage;
- Access must be limited to personnel performing the authorized import;
- The imported data must be verified appropriately; and
- The temporary local file should be securely deleted after successful import and verification unless retention is required.

The pharmacy recognizes that this temporary export stage occurs on a staff computer and is therefore outside the direct security controls of the application.

---

## 16. Network & Communications Security

Systems containing health information must use reasonable technical safeguards appropriate to the sensitivity of the information.

The application currently uses technical controls including:

- HTTPS for encrypted communications;
- Helmet Content Security Policy (CSP);
- Rate limiting; and
- Server-side security controls.

These controls must be maintained as the application evolves.

**Electronic Communications.** Personnel must:

- Verify recipients before sending sensitive information;
- Use approved systems;
- Avoid unnecessary health information in SMS;
- Avoid transmitting patient records through personal accounts;
- Protect email and application credentials; and
- Report suspected misdirected communications.

Patient communications should contain the minimum information reasonably necessary.

---

## 17. Access Controls

Access to health information is based on role and business need.

**Application Administration.** The Pharmacy Manager is currently the only individual with an Admin profile in the application. Other pharmacy staff do not have administrative privileges. Server-side authorization controls are used to enforce permissions and must not rely solely on the application's user interface.

**Account Requirements.** User accounts must:

- Be assigned to an individual;
- Not be shared;
- Use appropriate authentication;
- Have only the permissions required for the individual's role.

**Staff Onboarding.** Before access is provided:

1. The staff member's role must be authorized.
2. Required privacy/confidentiality training must be completed.
3. An individual user account must be established.
4. Only required permissions should be assigned.

**Staff Offboarding.** When a staff member leaves or no longer requires access:

- Access must be reviewed;
- Relevant accounts must be disabled or removed;
- Remote access must be revoked;
- Pharmacy-owned equipment must be recovered where applicable;
- Credentials must be changed where necessary; and
- Access to third-party systems must be revoked.

**Administrator Change.** If the Pharmacy Manager's administrative responsibilities end, the pharmacy owner/authorized management must ensure that an authorized replacement administrator is established. The application prevents deletion of the final Admin account to avoid accidental loss of administrative access.

## 18. Monitoring & Audit

The application maintains an audit log of privacy-relevant activities, including (as applicable) actor, action type, relevant detail, and timestamp. Audit information is accessible to the application's authorized administrator.

**Audit Review.** The Pharmacy Manager/Privacy Officer is responsible for reviewing audit logs. Audit logs will be reviewed:

- At least monthly;
- Following a suspected privacy incident;
- Following a patient privacy complaint;
- Following a suspected unauthorized access event;
- Following a significant system issue; or
- When otherwise warranted.

**Escalation.** The following may trigger formal incident review:

- Unauthorized access;
- Access inconsistent with a person's role;
- Unauthorized disclosure;
- Repeated inappropriate access;
- Suspicious account activity;
- Unauthorized modification or deletion;
- Compromised credentials;
- Significant security vulnerabilities; or
- Any activity creating a material privacy risk.

Findings and corrective actions will be documented.

---

## 19. Incident Response

All staff must immediately report suspected privacy or security incidents to the Pharmacy Manager/Privacy Officer. Examples include:

- Wrong-recipient email or SMS;
- Unauthorized patient-record access;
- Lost device;
- Stolen equipment;
- Lost paper records;
- Compromised credentials;
- Malware or ransomware;
- Unauthorized disclosure;
- Inappropriate staff browsing;
- Unauthorized data export;
- Accidental disclosure; or
- Other suspected compromise of health information.

**Incident Response Procedure.** The Privacy Officer will:

**Step 1 — Contain.** Take reasonable immediate steps to stop further access, disclosure, or loss.

**Step 2 — Preserve Evidence.** Preserve relevant audit logs, emails, files, system records, devices, and other evidence.

**Step 3 — Assess.** Determine what happened; what information was involved; which individuals may be affected; whether information was actually accessed or disclosed; the potential risk of harm; and whether the incident is ongoing.

**Step 4 — Escalate.** Material incidents will be escalated to the pharmacy owner/authorized management.

**Step 5 — Notifications.** The Privacy Officer will determine whether notification is required to affected individuals, the OIPC, the Alberta College of Pharmacy, law enforcement, an insurer, a vendor/service provider, or other appropriate parties.

**Step 6 — Corrective Action.** Corrective actions may include password changes, account suspension, system changes, additional training, vendor remediation, policy changes, security improvements, or other appropriate measures.

**Step 7 — Documentation.** Each significant incident will be documented, including date, description, information affected, individuals affected, actions taken, notifications, root cause where determined, and corrective actions.

---

## 20. Business Continuity

Remedy Pills Pharmacy will maintain reasonable measures to protect the availability and integrity of critical systems and information during system outages, hardware failures, cyber incidents, network interruptions, hosting failures, power disruptions, or other business interruptions.

**Backups.** Critical application data must be backed up using approved hosting/provider backup capabilities. The Pharmacy Manager/Privacy Officer maintains documentation of the backup schedule, retention period, and restoration process with the hosting provider, and reviews this documentation as part of ongoing vendor management (see §10). Backups containing health information must be protected appropriately.

**Downtime.** If the application becomes unavailable, pharmacy operations should use appropriate downtime procedures to continue essential patient services. Information recorded during downtime should be reconciled with the electronic system once normal operations resume. Restoration procedures should be tested periodically where practicable.

---

## 21. Change Control

Changes to systems, applications, data flows, integrations, vendors, hosting arrangements, or operational processes must be reviewed where they may affect privacy or security.

The change owner should document: description of change; reason for change; information affected; users affected; vendor involvement; privacy implications; security implications; testing; implementation plan; rollback/contingency plan.

**Privacy Review Required.** The Privacy Officer must review changes involving: new health-information collection; new uses; new disclosures; authentication; authorization; audit logging; retention; patient data imports; electronic communications; new vendors; hosting/storage locations; or other material privacy changes.

The Privacy Officer will determine whether the change requires a PIA amendment, revised PIA, IMA/vendor agreement, security assessment, staff training, or other controls. Changes should be reviewed before production implementation.

Emergency changes may be implemented where necessary to protect patient safety, information, or system availability, but must be documented and reviewed afterward.

## 22. PIAs, Policy and System Review

The Privacy Officer will conduct a comprehensive privacy-policy review at least annually.

The review will consider: changes in legislation; changes in ACP requirements; changes to pharmacy operations; new vendors; new technology; new application features; security incidents; patient complaints; audit-log findings; risk assessments; PIA amendments; and lessons learned.

Policies will be updated where necessary. Material policy changes will be communicated to affected personnel.

## 23. Privacy Complaints

Individuals may raise privacy concerns or complaints with the Pharmacy Manager/Privacy Officer, who will:

- Acknowledge the complaint;
- Document the concern;
- Review relevant records;
- Investigate as appropriate;
- Determine whether a privacy incident occurred;
- Take corrective action where necessary;
- Communicate the outcome as appropriate; and
- Advise the complainant of applicable external review or complaint options where required.

Complaints involving potential unauthorized access, use, disclosure, loss, or security compromise will also be assessed under the Incident Response Policy (§19).

---

## 24. Employee Confidentiality

All personnel with access to patient information are required to maintain confidentiality.

Personnel must not:

- Share passwords;
- Share patient information unnecessarily;
- Access records without a legitimate reason;
- Photograph patient records using personal devices;
- Store patient information in unauthorized locations;
- Send patient information to personal email accounts; or
- Discuss patient information where unauthorized individuals may overhear.

Confidentiality obligations continue after employment or contractual access ends.

---

## 25. Third-Party Data Import Procedure

Where external patient information is imported into the application:

1. An authorized staff member obtains the required source export.
2. The export is stored securely on the authorized workstation.
3. The file is treated as Restricted Health Information.
4. The authorized user confirms that the import is appropriate.
5. Patient/data confirmation procedures are completed before import.
6. The data is uploaded through the approved application process.
7. The imported information is checked for successful processing.
8. The temporary export is securely deleted when no longer required.

Any import problem or suspected incorrect patient association is reported immediately. Only authorized personnel may perform patient-data imports.

---

## 26. Policy Exceptions

Any proposed exception to this manual must be reviewed by the Pharmacy Manager/Privacy Officer.

Exceptions must be:

- Justified;
- Documented;
- Time-limited where possible;
- Approved by authorized management where appropriate; and
- Reviewed for privacy and security implications.

An exception must never be used to bypass a legal requirement.
